# GeneEd product purpose

## Purpose
Help curious people make sense of genomics and microbiology by connecting an explanation to a decision, an experiment and an evidence-based conclusion.

## Audience
Curious beginners, students revisiting foundations and learners interested in deeper biological context. The MVP provides shared foundations with optional depth; it does not claim complete content for every expertise level.

## Problem hypothesis
A learner may recognize vocabulary but struggle to use it to interpret unfamiliar evidence. This is a design hypothesis, not a finding from completed user research.

## Three ways in
1. Learn: plain-language concepts, diagrams, glossary and deeper notes.
2. Practise: retrieval questions with reasoning and targeted review.
3. Experiment: make a prediction, inspect a model and explain its assumptions.

## Demonstrable outcomes
- Distinguish gene from genome.
- Identify a sequence substitution without inferring an unsupported effect.
- Compare bacteria, viruses and microscopic fungi.
- Explain a recessive phenotype in a simple Mendelian model.
- Calculate a modeled doubling population and recognize limits.

## Design principles
- Curiosity before jargon: open with a question grounded in a visible example.
- Explanation before interpretation: distinguish observation, model and inference.
- Layer the depth: make introductory content usable while keeping additional context available.
- Feedback should lead somewhere: missed concepts produce useful review links.
- Keep scientific limits visible: label synthetic sequences, artificial growth assumptions and unfinished courses.

## Working flow
Discover → select way to explore → topic or investigation → response → explanatory feedback → suggested review or next activity.

## Evidence available today
Browser QA can establish that interactions work, scores calculate and local completion persists. It cannot establish learning effectiveness or validate audience needs.

## Proposed research
Test with a small mixed group of curious non-specialists and introductory biology students. Ask participants to interpret a new sequence difference and a new cross before and after using the product. Observe vocabulary barriers, incorrect inferences and whether review links help. Treat any learning claims as unverified until this work is actually completed.
